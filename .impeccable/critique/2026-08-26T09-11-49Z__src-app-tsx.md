## Design Specificity Verdict

The cold neutral device-lab dossier, inspection sheet, mono evidence labels, signal orange, and real-device proof are tied to an Android candidate. The composition still becomes resume-like in the work section because it has no real work artifacts. Detector result: zero findings in `src/App.tsx`. Browser evidence showed no horizontal overflow, console errors, or console warnings at tested desktop and mobile sizes. Mutable overlay injection was unavailable, so no visible overlay was claimed.

## Overall Impression

The page establishes a credible Android specialization quickly and avoids generic portfolio styling. Its biggest opportunity is to make engineering work, decisions, and verification lead the experience instead of asking project prose to carry all proof.

## What's Working

- Android, Kotlin, Compose, biometric SDK experience, and 10+ device testing are legible in seconds.
- The square dossier framing, precise label system, restrained orange, and neutral themes form a coherent visual world.
- Confidential work is described honestly; CV, source, live product, contact, focus states, reduced motion, and responsive navigation are present.

## Priority Issues

- **P1 - Work artifacts are missing.** Recruiters cannot assess UI quality or architecture without leaving the page. Add real screenshots, annotated flows, or architecture evidence when supplied. Do not fabricate mockups.
- **P1 - Case studies need engineering judgment.** Reframe verified content as challenge, decision, implementation, and verification where the CV supports it.
- **P2 - Best evidence appears too late.** Place flagship work immediately after the hero and compress or move About.
- **P2 - Specificity relies more on copy than form.** Use Android-specific evidence structures such as compatibility scope and implementation decisions without decorative fake devices.
- **P2 - Some trust language outruns displayed proof.** Rename Production Experience to Industry Experience and keep device testing claims scoped to IriTech.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan:** Acronyms such as MVVM, JWT, SDK, and TiDB may require domain familiarity; dense project prose makes proof harder to prioritize.
- **Riley:** Strong words such as verified and production invite scrutiny when no on-page test artifact or outcome is visible. External links carry much of the proof.
- **Casey:** Mobile touch targets and CTAs are strong, but oversized headings and long text case studies create a very tall interruption-prone scroll.

## Minor Observations

- The desktop profile sheet has unused vertical space.
- Skills repeat technology already documented in project evidence.
- Identical accent treatment for labels, dates, and the primary action weakens semantic distinction.
- Demo availability should be explicit per project.

## Questions to Consider

- If technology names disappeared, what artifact would still prove Android craft?
- Which real artifact would make the 10+ device claim immediately credible?
- Should recruiters experience engineering judgment or read an expanded resume?
